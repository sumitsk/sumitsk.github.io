Sumit Kumar

Staff Software Engineer at Aurora Innovation · Mountain View, CA

I work on generative behavioral simulation at Aurora Innovation. I built Aurora's first ML-based Scene Generator, a transformer model that produces human-like actor trajectories from keyframe inputs. I also lead the synthetic data pipeline that uses it to turn real driving logs into real-hybrid synthetic training and evaluation scenarios for the motion planner.

From 2023 to 2026 I led and managed the Intent Prediction team in Behavior Planning. The team's Scene Forecaster model was the forecasting engine for Aurora's commercial launch and runs on every fully autonomous Aurora vehicle. I hold an MS in Robotics from Carnegie Mellon University and a B.Tech in Mechanical Engineering with a minor in AI from IIT Kanpur.

Research projects

  • Recurrent Neural Processes for Spatiotemporal Prediction

    MS thesis, CMU, 2019 · Advisor: Prof. Katia Sycara

    A deep latent variable model that predicts over space and time from sparse, irregular samples and reports the uncertainty of each prediction. It beat Gaussian process baselines on accuracy and calibration across several benchmarks.

    Thesis

  • Learning Hierarchical Policies in Dynamic Environments

    Advisor: Prof. Ruslan Salakhutdinov

    A hierarchical meta-RL framework for sparse-reward locomotion where friction and body shape change. MAML-trained sub-policies, sequenced by a high-level policy, reached up to 2x the task success rate of pre-trained ones.

    Report

  • Deep Reinforcement Learning for Sparse-Reward Manipulation

    Advisor: Prof. Matt Mason

    Prioritized Hindsight Experience Replay adds TD-error prioritization and importance sampling to HER. On 7-DOF Fetch pick-and-place and push tasks, it learned faster and reached higher success rates than standard HER.

    Report

  • Estimating Configuration Space Belief for Motion Planning

    Personal Robotics Lab, 2016 · Advisor: Prof. Siddhartha Srinivasa

    A belief model over a robot's configuration space that uses past collision checks to predict which unchecked configurations are free. A topological method was more accurate than kNN and cut the collision checks needed in planning.

    Paper
